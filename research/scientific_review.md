# Internal AI-assisted scientific review of Warp Drive Wind Tunnel

18 November 2026. Reviewed simulator source revision `0b92ad7`, research scripts, packaged reference tables, viewer code and documentation. This is an independent computational review by AI reviewers, with analytic counterexamples, independently implemented numerical comparisons and primary-source checks. It has not undergone external journal or specialist review.

**Assessment.** The built-in Alcubierre and Natário presets have strong numerical support for the stress fields at the benchmark parameters. The static matter-sourced shell also passes an independent equilibrium integration. The custom-shell center and minimum-density displays can return physically incorrect results. The second-order clock numbers are reproducible within the stated spherical projection. A full rotating kinetic solution, its exterior quadrupole, stability, and a mechanism for propulsion remain open.

The table records findings against the reviewed revision. Subsequent corrections and their regression checks are described in the README. A separately supplied Claude-assisted review reports additional tensor and geodesic spot checks. Its underlying scripts and point tables were unavailable for replay.

## Findings that affect interpretation

| Priority | Finding and evidence | Correction and acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | The custom-shell center can hide both matter and a singularity. In `dist/physics.js` (`validate`, `customProfile`, `shellStress`), the code assigns zero central stress. For regular mass $m=a r^3$, Einstein’s equations give $\rho(0)=3a/(4\pi)$ and $p_r(0)=p_t(0)=-a/(4\pi)$. For $a=0.001$, the exact density is 0.000238732 in geometric units. At wall scale 0.37, the code gives 0.00090482 at radius 0.0001 and zero at the origin. The validator also admits `0.001*r^2`, whose density diverges as $1/r$. | Evaluate the finite limit of $m/r^3$ and its near-center behavior with a scale-aware check. Flag inconclusive limits. Test regular cubic, hollow, and singular quadratic profiles at zero and on both sides of the derivative step. |
| High | The reported minimum Eulerian density and both resolution-study minima can be fictitious. In `dist/compute-core.js`, `sample` starts the minimum at zero. Every cell of a grid sampling the regular positive-density cubic example above has positive density, yet the readout returns zero. This defect is independent of the exact-origin defect. | Initialize the measured minimum from the data. Compare both readouts to the minimum of the exported cells for a strictly positive profile. Keep color normalization separate. |
| Medium | **Shift speed** plots the norm of the prescribed lab-frame bubble flow. For the comoving metric the ADM shift is $v\mathbf e_x-\mathbf u$. At the Alcubierre center with $v=0.65$, the plotted field is 0.65 while the comoving shift is zero. The clock calculation already uses the relative velocity. See `dist/physics.js` and `dist/index.html`. | Label the plotted field “Bubble flow (lab frame)” and give the comoving expression next to the metric. Verify the center and far exterior in both frames. |
| Medium | Clock rates need an explicit observer. The main panel computes $d\tau/dt=\sqrt{-g_{tt}}$ for a fixed coordinate position. A custom profile need not have a normalized flat exterior, so its value cannot always be identified with seconds per distant lab second. The rotating research viewer instead plots $1-N$. For a held clock its rate is $\sqrt{N^2-r^2\sin^2\theta\,(\beta^\phi)^2}$. The distinction first enters at quadratic order and vanishes on axis. | Identify the time coordinate for custom profiles, label the research field as lapse deficit, and specify when it equals the held-clock deficit. Test a nondecaying custom profile and an off-axis rotating point. |
| Medium | Map legends and maps encode magnitude differently. In `dist/app.js` and `dist/research-view.js` the raster brightness varies as normalized magnitude raised to 0.65, while the legends interpolate colors linearly. A value at 10% of full scale has about 22.4% of the endpoint brightness. The research map also prints “z = 0” on second-order maps reconstructed from a spherical average. A genuine equatorial slice would contain the unresolved quadrupole. | Render legends with the same transfer function or supply a calibrated numerical key. Label the second-order image “spherical average” on the plot. Verify colors at zero, 10%, 50% and full scale. |
| Medium | The second-order note describes the exported peak as direct density forcing. In `research/vlasov_second_order.py` it includes the geometric term $q$. At bias 0.02 the matter-only peak is $3.19452\times10^{-10}$ and the combined Hamiltonian-source peak is $3.19241\times10^{-10}$ in inverse squared length units. The printed values round alike, but the quantities have different physical meanings. | Name both terms and compare them separately in the generator. Neither number is an exterior quadrupole moment. |
| Documentation | The rotation note contains backspace bytes in two beta symbols, and the equilibrium note contains a carriage return inside a density symbol. Several inline equations use plain parentheses around TeX. These defects obscure the quantity being differentiated or plotted. The independent prescribed-rotation tensor check also compares absolute currents, leaving the sign convention unchecked. | Repair the math markup, inspect the rendered equations, and compare signed $j_{\hat\phi}=-T_{\hat0\hat\phi}$ at points on both sides of a current reversal. |

Two additional display cases matter for interpreting an apparent boundary. At speed 0.99, radius 3 and wall scale 0.18, the central Alcubierre ray exhausts 2,300 steps after moving from x = −6.169 to −5.961, before reaching the wall. Its Hamiltonian drift is only about 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶. The plot should identify time-limited trajectories. In the wide shell map, corners can reach radius 8.49 while profile data end near 7. The viewer repeats the last sampled value there, producing a false constant exterior for lapse fields. Mask those pixels or use a justified exterior solution.

The shell study cites an unversioned preprint for a reported Fuchs smoothing-tail energy-condition violation. The claim appears in versions 1 and 2. Version 3 studies elastic-shell support and clock shifts. Pin the earlier citation and identify the claim as a result from that version. ## Evidence by model and experiment

- **Flat, Alcubierre and Natário.** The flat-space ray error reproduced at $5.715\times10^{-11}$ length units. The shipped regression suite replayed both Warpax grids. On the finer grid the maximum absolute errors across the ten stress components were $2.31\times10^{-8}$ and $2.22\times10^{-7}$ respectively, with approximately fourfold improvement on halving the derivative step. This tests two metrics at one parameter set. Negative Eulerian density suffices for a WEC counterexample. For the NEC the relevant observable is the null contraction. Sampling 198 directions can find a negative witness; a positive sampled minimum cannot establish positivity for every null direction. [Benchmark details](benchmark_warpax.md) · [Alcubierre](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0009013) · [Natário](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0110086) · [Energy-condition analysis and hypotheses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03079)
- **Conformal pocket and ultrastatic shell.** The pocket uses an explicitly softened scale factor of four. Its proper lengths depend on the spatial conformal factor, so the dashed coordinate radius alone cannot establish interior volume or energy savings. An independent Warpax spot check of the conformal metric at six meridional points (speed 0.65, radius 2.1, wall scale 0.37) recovered both signs of wall density. Maximum absolute differences were $5.55\times10^{-15}$ in density and $5.33\times10^{-16}$ in expansion in geometric units. This check covers two scalar fields at selected points. For the ultrastatic shell, positive wall density coexists with radial tension outside the wall, where $p_r=-M/(4\pi r^3)$. The latter exterior differs physically from the vacuum Schwarzschild exterior of the Einstein cluster. The references to other proposals are literature context, with no numerical verification of those proposals implied. [Van Den Broeck](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9905084) · [Shell equations](positive_energy_shell.md)
- **Photon paths and spatial diagrams.** The stationary null Hamiltonian, common coordinate launch conditions and rotation of meridional rays are appropriate for these axisymmetric presets and this launch family. The 3D mesh represents the coordinate reference radius. An event horizon, an independently integrated general 3D beam, a camera image with light-travel delays, and measured photon flux would each require a different observable. The path markers encode integration steps; their spacing is unsuitable for inferring flux. Hamiltonian drift measures conservation and can remain small even with trajectory error. Compare positions at matched times in a step refinement.
- **Optical analogue.** For shell radius 2.1, wall scale 0.37, compactness 0.4 and launch offset 0.65 times the radius, a separate cross-check mapped the isotropic optical ray to areal coordinates using $r=nq$. Across 474 matched samples at step 0.025 the maximum position discrepancy with the spacetime ray was $3.19\times10^{-5}$ length units. The outgoing angle was about −10.64 degrees at the finite screen. Calling it an asymptotic scattering angle would require extrapolation. The graded medium reproduces ray geometry under the static optical assumptions; it supplies no gravitational source.
- **Static Einstein cluster.** The spherical equations, clock bound, and circular test-orbit stability criterion are consistent. The script reproduces a cavity lapse deficit of 0.105208650 and a guided-loop asymptotic delay of about $5.20\times10^{-11}$ seconds for the prescribed rotating example at the stated scale. The static cluster and the prescribed rotation are distinct hypotheses. The latter’s numerical stress checks sample a finite set, and the continuity argument establishes existence of a sufficiently small perturbation without assigning a global numerical threshold. [Study and derivation](positive_energy_shell.md) · [Einstein-cluster matter interpretation](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1756)
- **Static Einstein–Vlasov and evolution.** Independent integration in physical momentum with adaptive quadrature and a DOP853 radial solver gave outer radius 3.609598510, ADM mass 0.2595646314, particle rest mass 0.2671144822 and cavity deficit 0.1595467282. These agree closely with the project’s Simpson/RK4 implementation. The evolution’s two jointly refined runs and unperturbed control support a finite-time radial rebound for the tested initial data. They cannot isolate phase-space, time-step and field-grid errors or establish collective stability. A convergence result for another particle scheme does not automatically transfer. [Static shells](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/9810014) · [Particle-method convergence](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0209336) · [Evolution and resolution study](vlasov_evolution.md)
- **Counterrotation.** Tuning the linear angular momentum to zero permits interior frame dragging in this calculation. Opposite clock corrections under different second-order constraints are consistent because the two calculations vary different physical families. Replaying the second-order script gave changes in cavity deficit of $+8.87\times10^{-10}$ at fixed central cutoff and $-6.58\times10^{-9}$ at fixed particle rest mass. The 400/800 comparison also changes momentum quadrature from 24 to 40 and holds the background solver fixed. Its 0.013% agreement is a joint refinement check, not an overall uncertainty bound. The displaced-cutoff test checks one wall point. The quadratic projection omits the coupled angular response of the metric and distribution. A deformed axisymmetric metric need not conserve total squared angular momentum, and the sign of the projected clock correction alone cannot demonstrate a realizable rotating system. [Rotation study](vlasov_rotation.md) · [Second-order equations](vlasov_second_order.md) · [Stationary rotating Einstein–Vlasov existence result](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5028)
- **Research tools and reproducibility.** The experiment JSON records settings, step, launch direction and observer convention. Field CSVs contain cell-center samples and photon CSVs contain coordinate-time trajectories. Preserve the JSON, simulator revision, units and observer with each CSV. The exported rows alone cannot reconstruct custom formulas or the physical length scale. The SI calculator rescales a fixed dimensionless geometry, so its mass and guided-light delays describe that rescaled benchmark. They do not estimate achievable engineering performance.

## Reproduce the principal checks

From the simulator checkout, run:

```sh
node verify.cjs
python3 -B research/cluster_shell.py
python3 -B research/check_stress.py
python3 -B research/vlasov_second_order.py
```

For the center defect, select the custom ultrastatic shell, use `0.001*r^3`, and compare the directional probe at the origin and the minimum-density readout with $3(0.001)/(4\pi)$. Repeat the origin test with `0.001*r^2` at wall scale 0.37. The finite positive-radius result should grow as $1/r$ in the latter case.

The independent Warpax regeneration reproduced all four packaged CSVs and the summary byte for byte. A fresh full spherical particle evolution, an unrestricted axisymmetric evolution, and a survey of every possible user formula were outside the verification performed here. The independent optical and equilibrium comparisons above were reviewer spot checks. A subsequent release can turn them into permanent regression fixtures.
